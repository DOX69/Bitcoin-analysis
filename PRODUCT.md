# Product

## Register

product

## Users

Bitcoin-focused investors and traders who want one trusted workspace for market analysis and personal position tracking. They monitor spot purchases and directional long or short positions, compare entries with market context, and review exposure without the noise of a multi-asset crypto platform.

The primary context is a focused desktop analysis session. Mobile supports quick checks, layer changes, position review, and follow-up decisions while preserving the same mental model.

## Product Purpose

Bitcoin Analysis combines Bitcoin market intelligence, a personal transaction ledger, portfolio exposure, and risk context on a shared timeline. It helps users understand what changed, where their purchases and positions sit within that change, and whether their current exposure still matches their plan.

The product succeeds when a user can:

- assess the current Bitcoin market without switching between unrelated tools;
- reconcile spot purchases and long or short positions against market history;
- reveal or hide analytical layers without losing chart context;
- trace every important metric to its source and freshness state;
- review risk and past decisions before recording the next action.

The product supports analysis and record keeping. It does not present itself as an exchange or imply that displayed analysis guarantees an outcome.

## Brand Personality

**Sovereign, exact, focused.**

The product speaks with calm confidence. Copy is concise, factual, and written in English. It respects experienced Bitcoin users without assuming that density must become clutter. Financial values, source states, and risk labels should feel precise enough to trust at a glance.

Reference qualities:

- CoinMarketCap for fast market scanning and familiar data hierarchy;
- TradingView for direct chart manipulation and analytical controls;
- BGeometrics for Bitcoin-specific depth and long-horizon context;
- prototype A for its reconciliation-led information architecture;
- prototype C for selectable analytical layers and timeline waypoints;
- prototype D for its premium command-center character and disciplined Bitcoin identity.

## Anti-references

- Generic multi-asset crypto dashboards where Bitcoin competes with token rankings, promotions, or market noise.
- Exchange interfaces that prioritize order placement, leverage promotion, or urgency over analysis and risk context.
- Neon trading-terminal cosplay, decorative glass panels, excessive glow, and motion without a state change.
- Gamified profit language, confetti, streaks, fear-of-missing-out prompts, or implied financial certainty.
- Dense screens with no clear reading order, unexplained indicators, or metrics detached from source and time.
- Generic AI or SaaS dashboard patterns built around oversized hero metrics and interchangeable cards.

## Design Principles

### Reconciliation before spectacle

Use prototype A as the structural foundation. Market evidence, personal records, exposure, and source status form one accountable workspace. The interface should answer where a number came from and how it relates to the user's own history.

### One timeline, many analytical layers

Borrow prototype C's interaction model. Price, purchases, positions, on-chain signals, derivatives, and macro context can be toggled without changing the underlying timeline. Personal entries become waypoints that connect the chart to the ledger and position history.

### Bitcoin depth without crypto noise

Every primary workflow serves Bitcoin analysis or Bitcoin position tracking. Additional data earns its place by improving a Bitcoin decision, not by expanding asset coverage.

### Familiar controls, deliberate density

The product should feel immediately usable to someone familiar with strong financial tools. Standard navigation, chart controls, tables, filters, and disclosure patterns are preferred over novel affordances. Density is welcome when hierarchy and spacing keep the next action clear.

### Risk and provenance stay visible

Exposure, leverage, liquidation context, data freshness, and source completeness are first-class information. Positive performance never hides uncertainty or risk. Demo, delayed, estimated, and incomplete data must be labeled where it appears.

## Accessibility & Inclusion

Target WCAG 2.2 AA for the product interface.

- All workflows must be operable by keyboard with visible focus states.
- Text and essential data must meet contrast requirements across dark surfaces.
- Color may reinforce gain, loss, risk, or selection, but must not carry meaning alone.
- Charts need readable labels, tooltips, and a non-visual data alternative for essential values.
- Motion should communicate state, remain brief, and respect reduced-motion preferences.
- Desktop and mobile must preserve task order, labels, and access to essential portfolio and risk information.
